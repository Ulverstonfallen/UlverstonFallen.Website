1901 Census living at 3 Oubas Hill were John L. Holmes (34) Dock Labourer, born Colton, Lancs; Isabella Holmes (33) born Ulverston; Joseph B. Holmes (5) born Ulverston; Myles Holmes (4) born Ulverston; John L. Holmes (3) born Ulverston.

By the time of the 1911 Census the family has grown. Still at 3 Oubas Hill are John Lewis Holmes (49) Iron Ore Miner, born Egton, Lancs; Isabella Holmes (43) married 15 years, seven children, all living, born Ulverston; Myles Holmes (14) Butcher’s Errand Boy, born Ulverston; John Lewis Holmes (13) Grocer’s Errand Boy, born Ulverston; Agnes Holmes (9) born Ulverston; Nelson Holmes (7) born Ulverston; Elinor (sic) Holmes (5) born Ulverston; James Holmes (3 month)(sic) born Ulverston; Agnes Holmes (51) Single, Visitor, Housemaid, born Newton-in-Cartmel.

From the Barrow News 9 December 1916

ULVERSTON PARENTS' BEREAVEMENT.

TWO SONS KILLED: ANOTHER SERVING.

The accompanying photo is that of the late Private Myles Bateman Holmes, Royal Fusiliers, second son of Mr. and Mrs. John Lewis Holmes, of 3, Oubas Hill, Ulverston, officially reported some time ago as wounded and missing, and now reported by the War Office and the British Red Cross Society as having been killed in action on the 27th July. Deceased, who was an “old boy” of the Dale-street Council School, joined the colours on Oct. 30th, 1915. At the time of enlistment he was in the employ of Mr. C. W. Hunter, of Barrow, serving his apprenticeship as a fitter. He went into training at Leamington Spa, Warwickshire, and became attached to the sapping platoon of his regiment. He paid his first and last furlough home last Christmas, and was drafted to France on the 4th Feb. last.

In his last letter home, dated July 22nd, 1916, he stated that he was “in the pink,” but, alas, little he thought that so soon after he would be called upon to pay the supreme sacrifice. He was only 20 years of age.

Deep sympathy is extended to Mr. and Mrs. Holmes in this, their double bereavement, their eldest son, Sergt. Joseph Bateman Holmes, 6th Battalion King’s Own Royal Lancaster Regiment, having died of his wounds at Mesopotamia on the 17th of May this year. One can only cherish the hope that the sorrow of the parents may be chastened by the assurance that their brave lads have nobly met their deaths serving their King and country.

Their third son, John Lewis Holmes, who is attached to the machine gun corps of the 3rd King’s Own, is now in training at Grantham.
