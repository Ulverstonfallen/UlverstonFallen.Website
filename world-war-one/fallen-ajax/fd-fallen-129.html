Age: 26

The Commonwealth War Graves Commission website also has information regarding this person

Frank Porter died on the first day of the Battle of the Somme.

From Ancestry we learn that Frank Porter, son of Robert & Jane Porter, was born on 8 November 1889, and baptised in Ulverston on 1 January 1890.

In the 1891 Census living at 32 Sun Street, Ulverston, are Robert Porter (40) House Painter, born Threlkeld, Cumberland; Jane Porter (34) born Blindcrake, Cumberland; John W. Porter (10?) Scholar, born Ulverston (as were all the following children); Robert I. Porter (8) Scholar; Charles Porter (6) Scholar; Frank Porter (1); and Ethel Porter (1). Presumably twins.

In the 1901 Census the family are at 47 Sun Street. The household is Robert Porter (50) House Painter, born Penrith, Cumberland; Jane Porter (44) born Cockermouth, Cumberland; John W. Porter (20) Apprentice Engineer, born Ulverston; Robert I. Porter (18) Commercial Clerk, born Ulverston; Charlie (sic) Porter (16) Apprentice Engineer, born Ulverston; Frank Porter (11), no occupation recorded, born Ulverston; and Ethel Porter (11) born Ulverston.

By the 1911 Census the family (mistakenly transcribed as Porten) had moved to 26 Clarence Street, Ulverston, where lived Robert Porter (61) House Painter, born Threlkeld, Cumberland; Jane Porter (55) Married for 34 years, six children, five still living, born Blindcrake, Cumberland; Robert Irving Porter (28) Single, Clerk, Blast Furnace/Ironworks, born Ulverston (as were the following); Charley (sic) Porter (26) Single, Fitter, Gun Mounting, Shipbuilding; Frank Porter (21) Single, Clerk, Estate Agents; Ethel Porter (21) no occupation recorded.

In the 1921 Census the household, still at 26 Clarence Street, Ulverston, had changed. Jane Porter (65) Widow, Home Duties, born Blindcrake, Cumberland, is now the Head. Other members are Isaac Beetham Hutchinson (38) Son-in-law, Engine Fitter, Vickers Ltd., born Ulpha, Cumberland; Ethel Hutchinson (31) Home Duties, born Ulverston; Frank Hutchinson (2) born Ulverston, and probably named in memory of his mother’s twin brother; and Fred Hutchinson (6 months) born Ulverston.

From the Barrow News 22 July 1916

On Monday, Mrs. Porter, of 26 Clarence-street, Ulverston, received information that her youngest son, Lance-Corpl. Frank Porter, of the 1st King's Own Royal Lancaster Regiment, had been killed in action at the commencement of the big British offensive on July 1st. The intelligence was contained in the following letter, dated July 11th, from Pte. F. Martindale, 1,353, 1st Platoon, "A" Company, 1st King's Own Regiment:— Dear Mrs. Porter,—It is with deep regret I write these few lines informing you of the death of your son Frank, which occurred on 1st July, 1916. He was in the same platoon as myself, and we were very great chums, and I think it my duty to write to you. He was well liked by all in the company, and by his death we have lost a good comrade. He died doing his duty - leading his section in action, and I can assure you he got a good name and a proper burial, and the platoon and myself share our sympathy with you in your sad bereavement.

The deceased soldier, who was 26 years of age, was an "Old Boy" of the Ulverston National School, under Mr. J. S. Whitham. On leaving school, he entered the office of Mr. R. O. Neill Pearson, solicitor, and joined the old Volunteers, in which he served for three years. He was also a corporal in the new Territorial force for 12 months. He re-enlisted in the 1st Royal Lancasters about fourteen months ago, at that time being employed in the office of the Isle of Walney Estate Co., and during the past twelve months he had been frequently in the trenches in France. Much sympathy is extended to the widowed mother, who feels the loss the more acutely because her son had not had any furlough since his enlistment.
